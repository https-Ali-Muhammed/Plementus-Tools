## Authority and observed limit

The [approved SEC-01 policy](PHASE_04_SEC01_APPROVED_POLICY.md) requires non-renewable logical expiry exactly seven days after immutable `createdAt`. The [retention amendment](PHASE_04_SEC01_RETENTION_AMENDMENT_B.md) requires an attempt to delete expired records at the earliest permitted extension execution after expiry, before checkpoint use. The [cleanup design](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_CLOCK_DESIGN.md), [disconnected gate evidence](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_GATE_IMPLEMENTATION.md), [isolated Chrome evidence](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_GATE_BROWSER.md), and historical [CP4 review](PHASE_04_SEC01_CP4_READINESS_REVIEW.md) establish their bounded design and test scopes. None establishes OS clock integrity.

The disconnected `createCheckpointService` accepts an injected `now()` function. Its `time()` check rejects invalid values and wall-clock samples lower than an earlier sample in the same service instance. `checkpointEligibility` compares a valid sample with `createdAt + 604800000`. Those checks prove the rule for the supplied sample; they do not prove that seven days elapsed. Local wall-clock time can change. A backward jump across worker or browser restart may make an expired record appear eligible for longer. A forward jump may classify an unexpired record as expired and cause premature physical deletion. The seven persisted fields (`schemaVersion`, `runId`, `state`, `step`, `evidenceTier`, `revision`, `createdAt`) contain no trusted last-observed time or elapsed-time proof. Same-worker rollback detection loses its observation when the worker ends and does not detect every forward jump. Controlled clocks in Node and disposable Chrome test the service, not the OS clock. Browser-only time is not tamper-proof. Without a trusted external time reference, this architecture cannot guarantee exact seven-day elapsed-time expiry across restarts. That security gate remains **OPEN / BLOCKED**.

## Recommended candidate: B, with a retained activation gate

**PROPOSED / OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED:** Treat B as the conservative candidate because it denies use when the service cannot establish a sound time basis and avoids an irreversible expiry deletion on an uncertain clock. Selecting B alone would not supply trusted elapsed time or authorize activation. If the owner and security reviewer cannot approve a defensible resolution process and the retention conflict, retain C and **NO_GO**.

| Observation | Proposed interpretation and behavior |
|---|---|
| Normal worker restart | Routine lifecycle event, not confirmed tampering. In-memory wall-clock history and any monotonic comparison reset. A checkpoint may be used only if the approved trust criteria can establish eligibility; otherwise block it. |
| Normal browser restart | Also routine. Persisted `createdAt` survives, but it does not establish elapsed time. Reopening alone is not service execution; first actual service execution must apply the approved gate. |
| Detectable backward movement | A valid new sample below a prior same-worker sample is evidence of rollback or inconsistency. Block checkpoint use and expiry-based deletion; record only a generic, sanitized blocked result. |
| Unexplained forward movement | A same-worker wall-time increase inconsistent with available monotonic elapsed time is suspicious; the discrepancy threshold and handling are **OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED**. Block use and expiry-based deletion pending review. Across restarts, a plausible forward jump may be undetectable. |
| Unavailable clock | Invalid, missing or throwing time source cannot classify expiry. Block checkpoint use and expiry-based deletion; surface a generic blocked state. |
| Time unverified across restarts | Uncertainty, not proof of tampering. The seven fields cannot resolve it. Block use where approved trust criteria cannot prove eligibility. An apparently reasonable local timestamp alone is insufficient for an exact seven-day guarantee. |

**PROPOSED / OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED:** Keep a blocked checkpoint unavailable to recovery, transition and external action. A separately authorized operator path could inspect sanitized evidence, reconcile any uncertain external action against its actual result, and choose an independently justified safe disposition. If expiry cannot be established, the operator may not certify the checkpoint as unexpired or resume it merely by resetting time; abandonment or explicit authorized deletion requires a reviewed path that preserves required incident evidence and reports transaction completion. A new action, if still needed, needs fresh approval and target checks. It is not a replay of the uncertain action. The owner must define who may decide, what evidence is acceptable, how authorization is recorded without adding a checkpoint field, and whether any continuation is ever allowed when elapsed time remains unprovable. This draft supplies none of those identities, evidence sources, tolerances or timeouts.

## Retention conflict requiring a new decision

At the first actual service execution after expiry, approved retention requires a cleanup attempt before checkpoint-dependent work. If time is uncertain, the service can **deny checkpoint use** without deciding that the record has expired. It should **prevent premature physical deletion** based only on an untrusted forward clock. It can **attempt authorized cleanup** only after a defensible expiry classification and guarded transaction; an attempted scan with uncertain time must fail closed and must not be reported as completed deletion. It may **retain blocked records for resolution**, including malformed records, conflicts or uncertain-time records, but physical bytes can then persist beyond the next execution and perhaps indefinitely. A backward clock can also hide a due deletion. The approved closed-browser exception permits persistence solely pending the next execution and cleanup attempt; it does not clearly authorize prolonged retention caused by unresolved clock uncertainty.

**OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED:** Decide whether and how to reconcile next-execution deletion with an uncertain clock, including the permitted duration and disposition of blocked physical records, the authority for manual deletion, and what evidence can establish expiry without exposing or replaying work. Do not silently treat denied use as deletion, assume that a failed cleanup satisfies the obligation, or relax Option B retention. Until a reviewed policy resolves this conflict, persistent activation stays **NO_GO**. Even successful IndexedDB deletion or absence does not prove forensic erasure.
