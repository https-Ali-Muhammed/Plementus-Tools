## Authority, candidate, and evidence boundary

The binding [SEC-01 policy](PHASE_04_SEC01_APPROVED_POLICY.md), [combined A+B manual-resolution decision](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_B_MANUAL_RESOLUTION_DECISION.md), and [uncertain-time retention amendment](PHASE_04_SEC01_UNCERTAIN_TIME_RETENTION_AMENDMENT.md) permit exactly `schemaVersion`, `runId`, `state`, `step`, `evidenceTier`, `revision`, and immutable `createdAt`. Logical expiry is exactly `createdAt + 604800000` ms, without renewal. When time is sound, due physical cleanup is attempted at the next permitted execution before checkpoint-dependent work. An uncertain row remains blocked and is an outstanding owner-controlled physical-retention obligation, with no guaranteed maximum hold. Only explicit, case-specific project-owner approval permits manual destructive disposition when expiry cannot be proved; no delegation is approved. Approval does not prove elapsed time or authorize old-row use.

The [runtime clock-source decision](PHASE_04_SEC01_RUNTIME_CLOCK_SOURCE_DECISION.md) is **PROPOSED**. The [combined remediation](PHASE_04_SEC01_RUNTIME_CLOCK_COMBINED_REMEDIATION.md) and [stalled-wall/restart resolution](PHASE_04_SEC01_STALLED_WALL_AND_RESTART_RESOLUTION.md) describe the current disconnected candidate, not accepted production wiring. In [`time()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs), one synchronous `monotonic-before → integer-wall → monotonic-after` sample must be valid, ordered, and at most 1 ms wide; a missing monotonic source fails closed. A wall rollback, monotonic regression, or forward excess beyond bracketed monotonic advance plus 1.1 ms latches a generic block. The forward check compares both the preceding sample and the first service-instance sample. [`eligibilityFor()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) requires both wall expiry and a same-instance lower bound of `current.before - creation.after - 0.1 ms >= 604800000` before automatic expiry; disagreement in either direction latches blocking and retains the row. [`checkpoint-policy.mjs`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-policy.mjs) classifies the exact wall boundary but does not establish clock trust. A new service cannot inherit a persisted row's volatile creation bracket; its global cleanup scan blocks that row and fresh creation. No later sample, restart, clock reset, retry, or manual approval clears that uncertainty.

Evidence below is historical and keeps its original scope. The [clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md) observed two short 20,000-sample runs; the [load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md) observed two final runs of three 20,000-sample scenarios. The [safer-sampling assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_SAFER_SAMPLING_DESIGN_ASSESSMENT.md) concluded that no alternative with equivalent detection and a measured false-block reduction is presently demonstrated. Synthetic Node and isolated-browser pass counts in those reports prove only their stated branches and local observations. This packet runs no new Node, browser, provider, storage, or production tests.

### 1. Maximum 1 ms bracket width

- **Existing candidate behavior:** Reject any `after - before > 1` ms before a cleanup transaction; latch the service and block checkpoint operations. An attempted wide sample cannot be discarded or retried to regain authority.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`time()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) has `MAX_BRACKET_MS = 1`. [Clock-source verification, observations](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) reports **1 of 40,000** widths above 1 ms (4.4 ms maximum in run 1). [Load assessment, observations](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md#observations) reports **6 of 120,000** above 1 ms across two final runs, all under controlled CPU contention (7.5 and 2.0 ms maxima by run); an earlier exploratory run saw violations in every scenario.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** Ordinary scheduling can create an irreversible false block. These short, single-host bursts cannot estimate production operation frequency or bound all scheduler delays; a wider accepted bracket could hide movement inside its unobserved interval.
- **Security and availability consequence:** Strict rejection prevents an ambiguous bracket from authorizing deletion or use, but may block work and create an exceptional physical hold without a guaranteed end.
- **Recommendation:** **REVISE_ONLY_WITH_NEW_EVIDENCE** for production acceptance; keep the strict current cap unchanged.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Whether this measured and potentially larger false-block cost is acceptable in an explicitly supported environment. Any proposed wider cap needs its own detection-equivalence and threat decision.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Platform/workload coverage including contention and decision-point frequency, adversarial changes within delayed brackets, and an owner-reviewed burden/disposition assessment. Do not convert these sample counts into a production rate.

### 2. Maximum 1.1 ms forward discrepancy

- **Existing candidate behavior:** Reject wall advance greater than `current.after - previous.before + 1 + 0.1` ms or `current.after - first.before + 1 + 0.1` ms. The 1.1 ms is detection allowance only; it contributes **zero** elapsed time to expiry.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`time()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) implements both expressions. [Clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) reports 0 excesses among valid consecutive pairs across two runs (maximum 1 ms) and 0 among first-to-current pairs in run 2 (maximum about 0.4 ms; not measured in run 1). [Load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md#observations) reports 0 excesses among comparable pairs in six scenarios; pairs excluded by wide brackets are **not passes**.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** No controlled wall adjustment tested attack detection. A within-allowance change, including repeated small changes within the first-to-current bound, may evade it. Coarsened timers or shared pauses may invalidate the assumed bound.
- **Security and availability consequence:** An undetected forward shift can make the wall look expired, although the separate lower-bound expiry check prevents deletion unless it too passes. An unexplained excess blocks legitimate work and retains records.
- **Recommendation:** **REVISE_ONLY_WITH_NEW_EVIDENCE** for production acceptance; preserve 1.1 ms in the disconnected candidate.
- **Required owner/security decision:** State the clock-adjustment threat and whether the residual detection gap is acceptable; approve any revision only after measured and adversarial evidence.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Controlled manual/NTP-like changes, repeated sub-allowance adjustments, coarsened timers, contention and sleep/wake on each proposed platform, plus analysis of what a common-mode clock fault can evade.

### 3. Assumed 0.1 ms monotonic timer precision

- **Existing candidate behavior:** `TIMER_PRECISION_MS = 0.1` extends the forward detection bound and is subtracted from the same-instance expiry lower bound; fractional monotonic readings remain fractional.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`checkpoint-service.mjs`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) defines and uses the constant. [Clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) saw the smallest positive `before` step near 0.1 ms in both runs, with 18,009 and 17,861 fractional readings. [Load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md#observations) saw about 0.1 ms smallest positive steps in each of six scenarios.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** Observed granularity is not a guaranteed precision or error bound. Privacy settings, browser versions, OS and sleep behavior may coarsen or alter it; zero measured bracket width proves no precision bound.
- **Security and availability consequence:** A too-small margin can overstate conservative elapsed time or misclassify discrepancies; a coarsened platform can also false-block and prolong retention.
- **Recommendation:** **UNACCEPTABLE_OR_UNRESOLVED** as a production-wide assumption.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Specify supported timing environments and the acceptable conservative precision proof; reject production acceptance where it cannot be supplied.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Version- and setting-specific timer resolution/precision characterization with a security argument for the lower-bound subtraction, including coarsening and lifecycle conditions. Short minimum-step observations alone are insufficient.

### 4. Consecutive and first-to-current comparisons

- **Existing candidate behavior:** Compare every valid sample with the prior sample and the first sample of the same service instance. Preserve anchors and latch any excess; no first-anchor reset after a suspicious sample.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** Both checks appear in [`time()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs). The [combined remediation](PHASE_04_SEC01_RUNTIME_CLOCK_COMBINED_REMEDIATION.md#problems-and-changes) records why the first-to-current guard was added for cumulative small advances. [Clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) includes first-to-current results only for run 2; [load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md#observations) includes both metrics across six scenarios.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** These comparisons do not detect every sub-allowance adjustment, same-direction drift under the bound, shared pause, or unobserved restart interval. Wide-bracket exclusions are not evidence of safe pairs.
- **Security and availability consequence:** The first anchor constrains cumulative positive discrepancy within one service life; an anomaly blocks globally. Extra sample attempts may increase false-block opportunities.
- **Recommendation:** **RETAIN_CANDIDATE** as the strict comparison structure, without treating it as sufficient clock assurance.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Whether its residual undetectable movement and operational cost meet the stated threat model.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Adversarial cumulative-adjustment and scheduling cases at every decision point, including prior/first anchor persistence and no reset after failure, on the proposed supported platforms.

### 5. Wall rollback and monotonic regression

- **Existing candidate behavior:** A wall reading below the previous wall or a new `before` below the previous `after` latches a generic block; malformed, missing, reversed or out-of-range readings likewise block. No later plausible reading clears it.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`time()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) contains these guards and latch. [Clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) and [load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md#observations) each observed **0** such events in their short active runs. The [stalled-wall/restart resolution](PHASE_04_SEC01_STALLED_WALL_AND_RESTART_RESOLUTION.md#authority-and-defect) reports injected Node/browser guard coverage.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** Natural runs with zero events establish no detection rate. Rollback and correction between samples, and any change across a restart, can evade observation.
- **Security and availability consequence:** Detected rollback cannot extend old-row eligibility, and detected regression cannot establish expiry; a false detection blocks access and cleanup.
- **Recommendation:** **RETAIN_CANDIDATE** as a fail-closed rule.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Accept the observable-event threat boundary and decide whether additional controls are needed for between-sample or cross-restart changes.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Controlled rollback/regression and correction cases on proposed environments, with unchanged-row, whole-pass abort, and permanent-latch results; separately assess unobservable gaps.

### 6. Stalled wall, monotonic pause, and shared pause

- **Existing candidate behavior:** A wall that remains eligible when the same-instance monotonic lower bound reaches seven days latches blocking and retains the row. Wall advance beyond a paused monotonic bracket's 1.1 ms allowance also latches. If both clocks pause together or no samples occur, the service cannot infer the missing real time.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`eligibilityFor()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) checks eligible/expired disagreement independently of wall expiry. [Stalled-wall/restart resolution](PHASE_04_SEC01_STALLED_WALL_AND_RESTART_RESOLUTION.md#authority-and-defect) records the original `Missing expected rejection` regression and the corrected injected seven-day cases. The [safer-sampling assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_SAFER_SAMPLING_DESIGN_ASSESSMENT.md#options-and-security-reasoning) distinguishes monotonic pause and shared pause. Historical injected tests do **not** observe actual sleep/wake.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** A stalled wall is not necessarily detected before the seven-day lower bound; a shared pause or sleep behavior may leave no provable elapsed interval. No real sleep/wake or seven-real-day test exists.
- **Security and availability consequence:** Detected disagreement blocks premature deletion or stale use; unresolved pause can delay cleanup and require owner-controlled physical hold.
- **Recommendation:** **UNACCEPTABLE_OR_UNRESOLVED** as production clock-pause assurance; preserve the strict candidate response.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Define whether sleep/wake and shared-pause risk is in the supported product scope, and whether blocking/hold is acceptable there.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Real sleep/wake observations on each proposed platform, controlled wall/monotonic/shared-pause cases, and a security argument for any time interval that cannot be observed. No pause may be counted as elapsed merely because wall time later looks plausible.

### 7. Same-instance conservative seven-day expiry lower bound

- **Existing candidate behavior:** `checkpointEligibility()` reaches expiry only at wall `createdAt + 604800000`; automatic expiry deletion additionally needs `current.before - creation.after - 0.1 >= 604800000` in the original live service instance, without uncertainty. If either source says expired while the other does not, latch and retain. The 1.1 ms detection allowance never advances expiry.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`checkpoint-policy.mjs`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-policy.mjs) defines `RETENTION_MS = 604800000`; [`eligibilityFor()` and cleanup](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) enforce the second gate and committed transaction result. [Combined remediation](PHASE_04_SEC01_RUNTIME_CLOCK_COMBINED_REMEDIATION.md#problems-and-changes) and [stalled-wall/restart resolution](PHASE_04_SEC01_STALLED_WALL_AND_RESTART_RESOLUTION.md#authority-and-defect) report synthetic boundary and isolated-browser branch results. [Clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) explicitly calls seven real days **INCONCLUSIVE**.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** The 0.1 ms precision bound is unaccepted; the source clocks are not trusted authorities. No service has been observed continuously for seven real days, and MV3 worker termination makes this path potentially rare. Synthetic jumps cannot prove real elapsed time. Cleanup samples before its IndexedDB transaction and samples again after it; the later sample cannot undo a deletion already committed if clock trust changed during the transaction.
- **Security and availability consequence:** The two-source gate constrains early automatic deletion when its assumptions hold; any uncertainty blocks checkpoint use and can retain data beyond seven days under the approved exception.
- **Recommendation:** **UNACCEPTABLE_OR_UNRESOLVED** for production acceptance pending time-source and platform proof; keep the current lower bound.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Whether same-instance evidence under explicitly stated clock and device assumptions suffices for normal automatic expiry, and whether the likely low availability of that path is acceptable.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Conservative clock precision/integrity argument, boundary and disagreement tests including clock movement during the cleanup transaction, representative seven-real-day observation or a justified equivalent assurance, and analysis of worker survival and deletion/hold frequency. Do not infer any cross-restart elapsed time.

### 8. Restart uncertainty and retained old records

- **Existing candidate behavior:** Creation brackets live only in a service-instance `Map`. A new instance's global cleanup scan rejects any persisted row lacking that bracket before deletion or checkpoint-dependent work; an unresolved row also blocks fresh-run creation. No automatic resume, transition, authorization, retry, replay, deletion, or fresh-run admission occurs.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [`known`, `eligibilityFor()`, `cleanupPass()`, and `gate()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) implement the fail-closed path. [Clock-source verification, restart observations](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#restart-observations) observed a new worker after `ServiceWorker.stopWorker` and after browser-process restart with the original anchor absent; tab closure alone kept the same worker in that run. [Stalled-wall/restart resolution](PHASE_04_SEC01_STALLED_WALL_AND_RESTART_RESOLUTION.md#restart-behavior-and-its-limit) reports unchanged old rows and blocked API calls after worker/browser restart in isolated synthetic-worker tests.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** These are disconnected/test-worker observations. Production worker/recovery callers, real replay paths and actual operator authority are not wired or verified. Seven fields provide no trusted cross-restart interval.
- **Security and availability consequence:** Old rows are preserved and cannot authorize stale actions or guessed expiry. A retained row can indefinitely block new checkpoint work, leaving an outstanding physical-retention obligation.
- **Recommendation:** **RETAIN_CANDIDATE** as the current safety response; automatic cross-restart resumption is unresolved under this architecture.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Whether the product can operate with blocked restart recovery and owner-controlled disposition, or whether the architecture/product scope must change through a later explicit decision.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Worker/recovery integration and restart tests with a real guarded caller path, unchanged old rows, no replay/bypass, reliable case-specific authority, and reviewed disposition. Do not treat a new wall reading or time origin as a bridge.

### 9. False-block burden and exceptional physical holds

- **Existing candidate behavior:** One invalid or wide bracket or clock disagreement latches all checkpoint operations for that instance; an unresolved old row aborts the global cleanup pass and blocks even separate creation. The approved exceptional retention process keeps the row local, blocked, and outstanding until guarded owner-controlled disposition. No guaranteed maximum physical hold exists.
- **Exact supporting evidence:** [Clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md#clock-observations-and-candidate-thresholds) saw one ordinary wide bracket; [load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md#threshold-and-security-assessment) saw six under contention in final runs and earlier exploratory violations without contention. [`gate()` and `cleanupPass()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) enforce whole-pass blocking. [Combined A+B decision](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_B_MANUAL_RESOLUTION_DECISION.md#approved-operational-rules) and [retention amendment](PHASE_04_SEC01_UNCERTAIN_TIME_RETENTION_AMENDMENT.md#approved-exception-and-boundaries) approve the exception but do not implement owner identity, a manual path, or a maximum hold.
- **Known failure or coverage gap:** Sample counts are not an operational false-block rate. There is no implemented authenticated owner review, case tracking, incident hold/release, or demonstrated safe manual deletion of restart-uncertain records.
- **Security and availability consequence:** Fail-closed behavior prevents uncertain use/deletion but can halt the product and leave local data physically present for an unbounded period, subject to device/profile exposure controls and owner oversight.
- **Recommendation:** **UNACCEPTABLE_OR_UNRESOLVED** for production operations until the burden and disposition controls are accepted and verified.
- **Required owner/security decision:** Define tolerable blocked-work and physical-hold burden for the chosen supported environment, and approve a real case-specific authority/disposition process without delegation or inferred expiry.
- **Evidence before production acceptance:** Representative operational exposure model, sanitized outstanding-obligation tracking, authenticated owner/request/target/revision checks, conflict/abort/commit/absence evidence, incident evidence hold, and the device controls below. No failure or attempt may be reported as completed deletion.

## Reviewer decision options

**A. Conditionally accept the existing candidate for an explicitly limited environment.** The authorized owner/security reviewer must first identify the exact supported platform/device/profile and workload, state the clock-adjustment and pause threat model, accept the measured and residual false-block/hold burden, and require the row-specific evidence above. **B. Reject production acceptance and require additional evidence.** Keep the disconnected candidate as a fail-closed implementation artifact and production **NO_GO**. **C. Decide that the current browser-only architecture cannot meet the required assurance.** If the product requires trustworthy elapsed time across unobserved restarts, automatic resumption, or a guaranteed maximum physical-retention deadline, the present seven-field/local-clock architecture cannot prove those properties. Record a separate future architecture/product-scope and policy decision before any redesign. This option does not itself authorize a backend, remote clock, new persisted field, permission, or migration.

**Packet recommendation:** Choose **B now**. The evidence establishes useful fail-closed behavior and a real availability cost but cannot justify production acceptance of 0.1 ms precision, common-mode pause behavior, seven-real-day expiry, cross-restart elapsed time, or an operational hold process. Consider **A** only if an explicitly narrow environment and its security evidence can be supported; choose **C** if automatic cross-restart or bounded physical deletion is a non-negotiable product requirement.
