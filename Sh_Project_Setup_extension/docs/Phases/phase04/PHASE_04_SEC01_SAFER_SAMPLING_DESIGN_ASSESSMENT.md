## Question, authority, and evidence

Can ordinary scheduling-related false blocks be reduced without losing uncertainty evidence, weakening clock-anomaly detection, or changing seven-day retention? The [runtime clock-source decision](PHASE_04_SEC01_RUNTIME_CLOCK_SOURCE_DECISION.md) is proposed. The [combined remediation](PHASE_04_SEC01_RUNTIME_CLOCK_COMBINED_REMEDIATION.md) and [stalled-wall resolution](PHASE_04_SEC01_STALLED_WALL_AND_RESTART_RESOLUTION.md) describe a disconnected candidate, not accepted production clock behavior. The [clock-source verification](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md) saw one bracket above 1 ms in 40,000 ordinary worker samples. The [load assessment](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_LOAD_ASSESSMENT.md) saw six above 1 ms in 120,000 samples across two short runs; all six occurred under CPU contention. An earlier exploratory run also saw wide brackets without controlled contention. These are availability signals from one Linux/Chromium environment, not a production false-block rate or proof of clock integrity.

The current [`time()` service](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-service.mjs) synchronously reads monotonic-before, integer wall, then monotonic-after. It latches a generic failure for a missing/invalid/reversed sample, bracket wider than **1 ms**, wall rollback, monotonic regression, or forward discrepancy over **1.1 ms**, comparing both the preceding and first service-instance samples. The 0.1 ms precision assumption is used in the discrepancy and conservative expiry calculations. The service also samples before its global cleanup transaction, after that transaction, and at record operations; its volatile creation bracket is lost on restart. [`checkpointEligibility()`](../../../src/storage/checkpoint-policy.mjs) classifies wall time against immutable `createdAt + 604800000` ms but does not establish clock trust. A clean reading later cannot erase a failed reading or prove an intervening interval safe.

Binding constraints come from the [combined A+B manual-resolution decision](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_B_MANUAL_RESOLUTION_DECISION.md), [uncertain-time retention amendment](PHASE_04_SEC01_UNCERTAIN_TIME_RETENTION_AMENDMENT.md), and [security requirements](../../09_SECURITY_AND_SECRETS.md): retain exactly the seven permitted fields; never move the original creation anchor or `createdAt`; keep a detected uncertainty latched and the row blocked; attempt normal due cleanup at the next permitted execution before checkpoint-dependent work; and keep an unresolved row as an outstanding physical-retention obligation under owner control. No automatic deletion, resume, replay, retry, fresh-run admission, or global-gate bypass follows from a clock-design change. Case-specific project-owner approval for manual destructive disposition does not prove expiry. The **1 ms cap, 1.1 ms forward allowance, 0.1 ms precision assumption, and seven-day contract remain unchanged** in every option below.

## Options and security reasoning

### A. Retain the strict 1 ms fail-closed cap

This is the current disconnected behavior and the only presently executable rule. It gives **no reduction** in scheduling-related false blocks: one wide bracket latches the service, blocks the global cleanup gate, and can leave a row physically retained pending owner-controlled resolution. It preserves the present rollback, forward, consecutive and first-to-current checks; the first-to-current comparison limits accumulation of repeated small forward advances beyond the current allowance. Neither check proves detection of every adjustment within that allowance. A stalled wall against advancing monotonic time blocks even when the wall still calls a row eligible; a monotonic pause against advancing wall time blocks, while a shared pause or unobserved interval cannot establish real elapsed time. Quantized readings and precision worse than the assumed 0.1 ms can invalidate the candidate comparison or lower-bound proof despite a narrow measured bracket. A worker/browser restart loses the original bracket, blocks the old row, and leaves its retention obligation open.

Normal automatic expiry still requires both wall time at the exact seven-day boundary and the same-instance creation-to-current monotonic **lower bound** at or beyond seven days. The 1.1 ms detection allowance contributes zero elapsed time. An abnormally wide bracket is rejected before it can support eligibility or deletion. Further proof is still required for the numerical assumptions, supported platforms, clock pauses, operational false-block burden, and owner/security acceptance; existing synthetic and isolated-browser results are bounded evidence only.

### B. Change when or how samples are collected

**Promising hypothesis, not a proved security property:** fewer unnecessary sample attempts, or sampling at a less contended point, might reduce opportunities for a scheduler-delayed bracket. The current bracket already has no await between its three reads. Moving it across an IndexedDB await or using a reading taken earlier for a later security decision creates an unobserved decision gap. Sampling less often could miss a rollback, forward jump, small cumulative adjustment, stall, pause, or expiry boundary that the present before/after and operation checks could catch. More samples can instead increase the chance of a wide bracket. No measured alternative collection schedule or equivalent-detection proof exists.

Every **attempted** sample, including a wide or malformed one, must enter the trust decision. A retry, average, minimum-width choice, or later clean bracket cannot make the earlier uncertain interval disappear. One could collect later readings for diagnostics after a latch, but they cannot authorize checkpoint use, expiry deletion, or gate passage. A collection-only change cannot restore a creation bracket after worker/browser restart, cure coarsened timer precision or a shared clock pause, or change the seven-day lower bound. It must leave the same physical-retention obligation and global gate intact. Before an implementation experiment, security review would need an exact map of all current sample/decision points, a proof that no removed or moved point reduces anomaly or expiry coverage, and platform evidence of a real false-block reduction. Those prerequisites are absent.

### C. Use conservative interval-based clock reasoning

**Promising hypothesis, not a proved security property:** represent each reading as its full uncertainty interval, retain every bracket, and decide only where all values in the interval imply the same safe outcome. In principle, disjoint interval ordering might still reveal rollback or forward movement. In practice, a bracket wider than 1 ms means the wall reading occurred somewhere in a longer unobserved monotonic span. Accepting it can enlarge the interval in which a forward jump, rollback and correction, repeated small adjustment, stall, or pause hides. Discarding it or choosing a convenient endpoint loses evidence. Preserving the present first-to-current comparison and a permanent uncertainty latch is necessary but does not itself prove equivalent detection.

Timer quantization, including zero-width *measured* brackets, does not certify 0.1 ms precision. If actual precision is coarser, the proposed interval bounds and seven-day lower bound need fresh justification. At the expiry boundary, any interval that straddles seven days must block deletion and checkpoint use; the forward allowance cannot close the shortfall. A monotonic pause or restart gap cannot be filled with a new anchor. The global cleanup pass must abort as a whole on any uncertain row and keep its physical-retention obligation. A conservative interval model that continues past a wide bracket would require a formal detection-equivalence argument for the unchanged 1 ms security cap and 1.1 ms allowance, plus adversarial tests; no such argument or evidence is present. If it still rejects every bracket over 1 ms, it offers no demonstrated false-block reduction.

### D. Conclude no demonstrably safer replacement is available now

This is an **assessment conclusion**, not a new clock algorithm or policy. Operate with A's strict rejection, rollback/forward and cumulative first-to-current checks, stalled-wall/monotonic-pause blocks, precision assumption, wide-bracket latch, restart hold, exact seven-day two-source expiry gate, global cleanup gate, and outstanding physical-retention process. It provides no immediate reduction in ordinary false blocks. It also makes no unsupported claim that A detects every within-allowance change, that coarsened clocks are safe, or that a shared pause or restart interval can be timed. Owner/security must still decide whether the existing numerical candidate and its operational burden can ever be accepted. No replacement implementation or threshold change is justified by the present evidence.

## Recommendation and unresolved security questions

**Recommend D: retain A's strict fail-closed behavior and stop further speculative sampling design.** The observed wide brackets establish an availability cost. They do not show a way to reduce that cost while preserving every attempted sample, the unchanged numerical limits, anomaly coverage, and expiry safety. B changes the opportunity to observe anomalies; C may broaden a blind interval. Neither has a defensible equivalence argument from the current evidence. This recommendation is for the disconnected candidate only and is **not** numerical-criteria acceptance or production approval.

Unresolved owner/security questions are: Which browser/OS, privacy-timer and sleep/wake conditions are in scope? Is 0.1 ms a valid conservative precision bound there? What rollback, forward, cumulative-adjustment and shared-pause threats must the 1.1 ms comparison detect? What false-block and indefinite-hold burden is acceptable for the 1 ms cap? Is same-instance monotonic lower-bound evidence sufficient for normal deletion? How will outstanding physical holds, real project-owner authority, device controls and incident evidence be handled? The [approved policy](PHASE_04_SEC01_APPROVED_POLICY.md) and [security requirements](../../09_SECURITY_AND_SECRETS.md) leave runtime integration and final security acceptance open.

**Narrow implementation experiment: NOT JUSTIFIED now.** Required acceptance tests for such an experiment are **NOT APPLICABLE** until an exact candidate and detection-equivalence proof exist. If a later owner/security review supplies both and separately authorizes an experiment, its minimum test gate must compare original and candidate at every security decision; inject wide brackets hiding rollback, forward and repeated small adjustments; test wall stalls, monotonic/shared pauses, quantization worse than 0.1 ms, and transaction delays; assert permanent latches, unchanged rows and no cleanup-gate bypass; test just-before/at/after seven days without allowance credit; and verify worker/browser restart retains the old row and obligation. Isolated browser measurements would remain platform observations, not clock-integrity or seven-real-day proof.

**Next useful Phase 04 work package:** independent ChatGPT review of this assessment, followed by a bounded owner/security decision on the *existing* 1 ms, 1.1 ms and 0.1 ms candidate, supported platform matrix, same-instance expiry criterion and false-block/physical-hold burden. Stop speculative design pending that decision. No implementation, deployment, production-policy change, commit or push is authorized here.
