# SEC-01 retention amendment B: expiry and next-execution deletion

**Status: OPTION_B OWNER_APPROVED / IMPLEMENTATION_NOT_VERIFIED.** On 2026-10-29, the owner explicitly approved this documentation-only amendment to the [SEC-01 approved policy](PHASE_04_SEC01_APPROVED_POLICY.md). The original SEC-01 approval remains the authority for all unchanged security obligations. This amendment replaces only the original strict seven-day physical-deletion deadline; it does not authorize implementation, browser storage, permissions, provider operations or production activation. Phase 04 remains **IN_PROGRESS / BLOCKED**, progress remains **4/20 master phases and 18/72 micro-phases accepted**, and activation remains **NO_GO**.

## Owner-approved retention rule

- Logical expiry occurs exactly seven days after immutable `createdAt` (`createdAt + 604800000` milliseconds). Updates never reset `createdAt` or renew expiry.
- From the expiry boundary onward, a checkpoint may never resume, transition, authorize an action or trigger automatic retry or replay. Missing, corrupt, stale and uncertain checkpoints also fail closed; uncertain external writes still require operator reconciliation.
- Physical deletion of an expired checkpoint must be attempted at the earliest permitted extension execution after expiry, including startup or access, and before any checkpoint-dependent operation. If cleanup fails or conflicts, surface a safely redacted failure and block checkpoint-dependent operations until resolved. Never report deletion before transaction completion; verify absence after a successful deletion when feasible.
- When Chrome is closed at expiry, execution cannot occur. The owner explicitly acknowledges and permits physical persistence beyond seven days **solely pending the next available extension execution and cleanup attempt**. This is not permission to resume expired work or to ignore a failed cleanup. Deletion at the seven-day deadline while Chrome stays closed is not guaranteed.
- Explicit operator deletion remains supported. Delete completed or cancelled checkpoints after operator acknowledgment, or when they expire under the amended cleanup rule, whichever obligation arises first. Deletion uses guarded revision checks and fails closed on conflict or error.
- IndexedDB deletion does not prove secure forensic erasure from disk, backups or snapshots. No erasure guarantee is made.

The seven persisted fields remain exactly `schemaVersion`, `runId`, `state`, `step`, `evidenceTier`, `revision` and `createdAt`. Storage remains device- and browser-profile-local; sync, export, telemetry, external transmission, direct content-script storage access and prohibited sensitive data remain forbidden. Disk encryption, screen lock, protected profile, real operator approval, incident response, guarded runtime access and final security approval are unchanged activation requirements.

Clock rollback across worker or browser restarts remains unresolved. The approved fields do not reliably establish trusted time across restarts. This amendment approves neither another persisted field nor an external time service. Expiry enforcement and cleanup scheduling are **not verified** in production; the existing disconnected service exposes `cleanup()` but does not itself run automatically at startup/access or guard every checkpoint-dependent operation with cleanup.
