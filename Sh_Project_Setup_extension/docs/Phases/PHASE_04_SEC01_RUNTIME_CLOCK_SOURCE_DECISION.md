## Clock-source assessment

| Property | `Date.now()` | `performance.now()` in an MV3 worker | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning and precision | Unix-epoch wall time as integer milliseconds; the system clock can be adjusted and its effective precision may vary. | Floating-point milliseconds relative to that worker's `performance.timeOrigin`; normally monotonic, potentially coarsened. | Keep the persisted `createdAt` as the approved integer wall value. Retain monotonic fractions during comparison; rounding or flooring them to integers can add disagreement. Neither API is a trusted authority. |
| Sampling | Sequential calls observe different instants; a task may be delayed between calls or after an await. | A monotonic reading can bracket a wall reading within one running worker. | A raw `wallDelta > monotonicDelta` with zero tolerance treats sub-millisecond rounding, call order, timer granularity, and scheduling delay as suspicious. It can falsely block even with no clock change. |
| Suspension and sleep | Wall time normally advances while Chrome is idle or the device sleeps, but can be changed by the user/OS. | The specification expects sleep ticking; browser/OS implementations may pause it. A worker can also be terminated while idle. | A wall/monotonic disagreement after sleep is not proof of tampering, but it is insufficient evidence for automatic deletion. Delayed expiry is safer than premature deletion. |
| Restart | A new worker/browser can read a fresh wall value. | A new worker has a new time origin and loses in-memory anchors; `timeOrigin + now()` is not a trusted bridge over an unobserved gap. | Neither API proves elapsed time across an unobserved worker or browser restart. A plausible wall value after restart cannot establish safe expiry or safe continuation. |
| Clock movement | Rollback can falsely extend eligibility; a forward jump can falsely expire and delete early. | Within one live instance it can flag disagreement, but cannot explain every drift or cross-restart jump. | Detectable rollback, regression, or unexplained forward movement must block use and expiry deletion. |

Browser behavior above is grounded in [MDN's high-precision timing guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance_API/High_precision_timing), [MDN's `performance.now()` sleep note](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/now), [MDN's worker time-origin note](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/timeOrigin), and [Chrome's extension-worker lifecycle](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/service-workers/lifecycle). These platform descriptions motivate tests; they do not certify this machine's clock or every Chrome version.

## Proposed technical contract, subject to owner/security acceptance

1. **Sample and validate.** In the authoritative service, propose one synchronous bracket per decision: monotonic-before, wall, monotonic-after, with no await between reads. Reject a thrown, missing, nonfinite, negative, or out-of-range sample; require the wall value to be a nonnegative safe integer and the monotonic values to be finite, nonnegative, ordered, and within safe range. Retain fractional monotonic values in memory. A missing monotonic source is uncertainty, not permission to infer elapsed time from wall time alone. Reject an invalid bracket before opening a cleanup transaction.
2. **Compare within one instance.** Retain only in-memory prior brackets and, for a record created by that instance, its creation bracket. Reject wall rollback, monotonic regression, or an internally reversed bracket. For forward movement, compare the wall increase with the *maximum possible* monotonic elapsed interval between brackets plus a separately justified detection allowance for timer resolution and sampling uncertainty. An unexplained excess blocks. If that allowance cannot be supported by observed platform behavior and security review, leave it **UNDECIDED** and fail closed; do not silently use zero, an arbitrary constant, or a percentage of seven days. A too-wide or otherwise ambiguous bracket is uncertainty and blocks the decision.
3. **Latch uncertainty.** Once a sample or comparison fails, block checkpoint-dependent create/read/transition/delete/cleanup and expiry-based deletion for that service instance. A later plausible sample, reload, or clock adjustment cannot clear its blocked state. Return only a generic sanitized failure. This is detection and containment, not a diagnosis of tampering.
4. **Separate detection from expiry proof.** A forward-jump detection allowance never counts toward `604800000` elapsed milliseconds. For candidate normal expiry, require the wall sample to reach `createdAt + RETENTION_MS` **and** a conservative lower bound from the same continuously observed worker's creation bracket to the current bracket to reach at least `RETENTION_MS`, with no intervening uncertainty. Use the later edge of the creation bracket and earlier edge of the current bracket for that lower bound; do not round a fractional shortfall upward. If the monotonic clock pauses during sleep, this delays deletion. This is only a proposed browser-local operational criterion, not proof against clock manipulation or platform faults; owner/security must decide whether it is sufficient for normal cleanup. Before the boundary, a known same-instance record may be classified eligible only while the accepted clock criterion holds. Expiry is never renewed by an update.
5. **Treat persisted restart records as uncertain.** With no surviving in-memory creation bracket, a new worker cannot classify a pre-existing row as safely eligible or expired using only the seven fields. Keep it blocked and physically retained; do not delete at a plausible wall-clock boundary, automatically resume, retry, replay, or infer a new trusted baseline. The first actual service execution must surface the outstanding retention obligation through the already approved sanitized owner-controlled process. Worker termination and full browser restart lose this evidence; closing a tab alone does not necessarily terminate the worker. None proves tampering. A continuous seven-day worker lifetime is unlikely under normal MV3 idle termination, so this conservative option may leave most persisted checkpoints for owner review.
6. **Keep cleanup and disposition distinct.** Under approved Option B, attempt normal physical cleanup at the earliest permitted execution **when expiry is safely established**, before checkpoint-dependent work; confirm transaction completion and feasible absence, and block on abort/conflict. When time remains uncertain, the approved exceptional hold applies: no automatic uncertain deletion, no guaranteed maximum physical hold, and no claim that a blocked scan completed deletion. Only explicit, case-specific project-owner approval can authorize manual destructive disposition when expiry cannot be proved, using reliable target/revision evidence and the guarded transaction procedure in the approved A+B decision. No delegation, clock reset, or automatic resolution is proposed. If identity/revision evidence is unreliable, retain the row pending the separately reviewed procedure.

The proposed bracket does not assign a numerical sampling-width limit or forward-discrepancy allowance. Such values require isolated-browser measurement across supported platforms, documented residual risk, and owner/security acceptance. Even accepted detection values cannot be used to shorten the seven-day lower bound.

| A. Keep the present zero-tolerance check | Smallest code change. An obvious forward jump blocks. | Sequential sampling, precision differences, or sleep can latch false blocks; a restarted row still cannot prove expiry. No real runtime source is selected. |
| B. Bracket samples and require an independent conservative elapsed lower bound (**recommended candidate**) | Reduces avoidable comparison ambiguity while preserving a strict deletion threshold and the approved uncertain-time hold. Stays inside the seven-field, local-only service. | Needs measured detection criteria and focused implementation; worker restart still blocks old rows, often for an unbounded hold. If those criteria cannot be justified, do not implement B as an accepted clock source; keep Phase 04 and production blocked. The approved manual-resolution policy already governs uncertain rows, but actual project-owner identity and request checks remain unimplemented.

## Security invariants

- Persist exactly `schemaVersion`, `runId`, `state`, `step`, `evidenceTier`, `revision`, and immutable `createdAt`; add no checkpoint field, backend, remote time source, permission, or arbitrary reset.
- Preserve logical expiry at exactly `createdAt + 604800000` milliseconds. No update, review, or tolerance extends it. An expired checkpoint never authorizes use.
- Preserve Option B next-permitted-execution cleanup when time is sound and the approved exceptional local hold while time or cleanup remains uncertain. Never automatically delete an uncertain row or claim a maximum hold.
- Preserve atomic IndexedDB revisions/transactions, whole-pass abort on conflict, completed-commit reporting, feasible absence checks, and generic sanitized errors. Missing, malformed, stale, and uncertain rows fail closed.
- Preserve no automatic resume, transition, authorization, retry, or replay from an uncertain checkpoint. A possible external write requires reconciliation before separately authorized new work.
- Preserve case-specific project-owner authority for manual destructive disposition, with no delegation. Owner approval does not prove seven elapsed days or authorize old-checkpoint continuation.
