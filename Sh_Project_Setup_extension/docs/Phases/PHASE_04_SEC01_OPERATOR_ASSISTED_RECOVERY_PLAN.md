## Recovery sequence and meaning

1. **Detect:** On worker startup or checkpoint access, attempt the required guarded cleanup and classify an uncertain persisted row. Missing, corrupt, legacy, stale, failed-cleanup, possible-external-write and time-uncertain cases fail closed; a plausible wall timestamp after restart does not prove expiry. Present only the generic operator state **“checkpoint blocked; owner review required”** and sanitized cleanup status **not attempted / outstanding / committed**.
2. **Block the old workflow:** Freeze its effective use regardless of the stored `state`. Do not transition, resume, replay, retry, infer success, refresh `createdAt`, or reuse its revision, evidence tier, or approval. Preserve the old row and any incident evidence; no direct IndexedDB edit or clock reset is a recovery method.
3. **Preserve retention:** Record the outstanding physical-retention obligation in the approved sanitized owner-controlled process, without adding a checkpoint field, exporting the checkpoint, or calling a failed attempt deletion. Trusted expiry still means `createdAt + 604800000` milliseconds and cleanup at the next permitted execution before checkpoint-dependent work. When expiry is unprovable, the approved exceptional local hold has no guaranteed maximum; no automatic uncertain deletion follows. A closed browser cannot execute cleanup.
4. **Reconcile external effects:** An authorized operator checks the actual outcome of every possible external write through a separately approved read/attestation route. Unknown outcome stays blocked. Record only minimal redacted decision evidence under an approved evidence procedure; the checkpoint cannot hold account, target, URL, raw error, screenshot, or provider data.
5. **Reverify the intended work:** Before any new action, independently establish current target/project, account, exact origin, environment, permissions and intended effect. Recheck after navigation, tab change and restart. If overlap with the old effect or another run cannot be excluded, stop for owner/security resolution; the seven fields do not identify the old target.
6. **Authorize fresh work:** Obtain a new, action-specific approval from an authority established by the future identity/approval contract. An ordinary operator may perform reconciliation and submit a request, but cannot grant project-owner destructive authority. The old approval, manual-deletion approval and a generic “continue” choice cannot authorize the new action.
7. **Create a separate run:** Only after an approved isolation gate admits it, create a new `runId`, immutable current `createdAt`, revision `0`, and independently obtained evidence. Atomic duplicate/conflict, state, expiry, authorization and target checks still apply. This is a new workflow; it is **not automatic resumption** of the old checkpoint.
8. **Keep the old case open:** The old row remains blocked and its retention obligation outstanding until a separately authorized, case-specific disposition commits and feasible absence verification succeeds. When expiry remains unprovable, only the verified project owner may approve manual deletion; no delegation is approved. If authority, target/revision evidence, incident hold, transaction outcome, or owner availability is unresolved, retain and block it.

## Global gate: proposed isolation and exact blocker

The current `gate()` cannot admit Step 7 after a restart: it scans every row, sees the old row outside `known`, latches failure, and never reaches the fresh run's transaction. Changing only the new run ID or adding a UI approval cannot fix this. Disabling cleanup, skipping an old row without classification, deleting it to clear the gate, or using the v1 repository would violate SEC-01.

**Candidate, conditional on owner/security acceptance:** keep one authoritative service and the same IndexedDB store keyed by `runId`. Before fresh creation, make a complete guarded scan that attempts due cleanup for rows with accepted time evidence, validates every row and primary key, and distinguishes a valid but restart-uncertain **held** row from a malformed/legacy row or a transaction failure. A held row is never treated as cleaned, eligible, or deleted. The service must confirm the approved retention obligation and reconciliation/identity/target authorization evidence through a reviewed trust boundary before allowing a distinct fresh `runId`. Recheck that evidence and all row classifications inside the admission transaction; serialize against concurrent creation/disposition with IndexedDB transaction rules. A failed scan, corrupt or legacy row, absent or stale authorization, unknown external outcome, target overlap, revision conflict, or unconfirmed obligation blocks admission. Subsequent operations still execute the required cleanup/classification gate; they never inherit an earlier pass or approval. The new row has its own creation clock anchor and full normal guards. No checkpoint schema field, backend, remote time service, browser permission, provider write, direct repository path, or automatic action is part of this candidate.

**Decision required before implementation:** the approved Option A direction does not itself authorize treating an uncertain held row as compatible with fresh checkpoint creation while the global cleanup obligation remains outstanding. Owner and security reviewer must approve the exact hold classification, obligation evidence and failure semantics, including whether a complete scan with an explicitly retained uncertain row satisfies “cleanup before checkpoint-dependent operations.” They must also approve a reliable way to establish old/new target non-overlap and reconcile external effects without persisting prohibited target/account data or trusting operator assertion alone. The seven-field row cannot prove either fact after restart. If those contracts cannot be supplied within approved privacy and local-only limits, **fresh creation remains blocked**; do not invent an exception. Corrupt/legacy rows lacking reliable identity or revision require their own reviewed disposition procedure and are not automatically promoted to held rows.

| Guarded IndexedDB | Keep exact v2 keys `schemaVersion`, `runId`, `state`, `step`, `evidenceTier`, `revision`, `createdAt`; immutable creation time; atomic expected-state/revision checks; transaction-complete deletion reporting; feasible absence checks; generic errors; and one service. No sync, export, telemetry, external transmission, direct content-script access, customer identifiers, credentials, account data, URLs, repository identifiers, screenshots, raw errors or new persisted metadata. The runtime clock-source proposal does not close them. No tolerance may extend logical expiry or permit uncertain deletion. A fresh run's local clock evidence cannot rehabilitate the old row's lost cross-restart evidence. Disk encryption, screen lock and protected browser profile remain activation prerequisites, not properties of IndexedDB.
