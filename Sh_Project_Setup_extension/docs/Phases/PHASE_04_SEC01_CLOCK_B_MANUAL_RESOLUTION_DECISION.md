## The retention conflict

The [approved SEC-01 policy](PHASE_04_SEC01_APPROVED_POLICY.md) sets logical expiry at immutable `createdAt + 604800000` milliseconds. An expired checkpoint cannot be used. Retention Option B calls for physical cleanup at the first permitted extension execution after expiry, before checkpoint-dependent work. The [cleanup design](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_CLOCK_DESIGN.md) and [isolated browser report](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_GATE_BROWSER.md) show bounded transaction behavior, not trusted OS time. A suspect clock cannot establish that expiry occurred; expiry-based deletion could destroy an unexpired checkpoint. A blocked scan, conflict or failed transaction can leave physical records after that first execution. Denying use does not delete bytes, and an attempted or queued deletion is not a completed deletion. The closed-browser exception covers waiting for execution; it does not clearly cover indefinite retention after an execution that cannot safely classify or delete a record.

No blocked record is usable. | Preserves the possibility of review, but does not make the old checkpoint resumable. | **Needs an amendment** for uncertainty or failed cleanup beyond the next execution; no silent exception. | **OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED:** Owner chooses the hold and security reviewer assesses exposure and disposition. Later evidence: blocked use, intact row after abort, sanitized outstanding-retention record, and a separately authorized final transaction. |

**Recommended browser-only default, not approved:** Use **A** while the owner decides a narrowly authorized **B** disposition for records whose expiry cannot be established. Block use and expiry-based deletion on uncertain time. Without the separate policy and case approval, deletion without trusted expiry is not permitted. Treat each unresolved record as outstanding retention, not as successful cleanup. Keep activation NO_GO until the owner approves the conflict amendment and a security reviewer accepts the resulting exposure or destructive-disposition rule. A clock reset, a plausible timestamp, a checkbox or manual approval cannot prove seven days elapsed.

## Proposed manual-resolution rules — all OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED

The operator would see only a generic **checkpoint blocked; authorized review required** state and whether cleanup is **unresolved**, **committed** or **not attempted**. No record contents, customer details, credentials, raw errors or unredacted timestamps appear in UI, logs or exports. The owner must designate who may authorize a disposition and how that authority is checked before implementation; this package names no role or person. The reviewer records a sanitized case identifier, observed condition, approved decision, target/revision evidence, reconciliation result when relevant, transaction outcome and any remaining physical-retention obligation through an approved evidence process **outside the seven-field checkpoint**. No customer or credential persistence, extra checkpoint field, telemetry or remote service is proposed.

| Condition | Operator view and required evidence before resolution | Proposed disposition and continuation rule |
|---|---|---|
| Detectable clock rollback | Generic time-trust block; preserve sanitized clock-comparison result and lifecycle context. | Block checkpoint use and expiry deletion. A clock adjustment alone cannot clear the block. Authorized reviewer chooses continued hold or a separately approved destructive disposition; no old-checkpoint continuation on an unproved interval. |
| Unexplained forward jump | Same generic time-trust block; preserve sanitized wall/monotonic discrepancy evidence only where the same worker observed it. No tolerance is chosen here. | Prevent premature deletion and use. Cross-restart jumps may evade detection. Reviewer cannot mark elapsed time trusted from a plausible current clock alone. |
| Worker/browser restart with unresolved time trust | Generic blocked recovery; record the actual worker/service execution trigger and loss of process-local clock history. Restart itself is not proof of tampering. | No automatic resume, replay or deletion based on apparent expiry. Hold for authorized review; fresh approval is required for any new workflow. |
| Missing or unavailable time | Generic unavailable-time block; retain only sanitized failure category and whether a transaction started. | Block use and expiry deletion. Do not infer expiry from `createdAt` alone. Retry a later check only as a new guarded attempt; no previously blocked request proceeds automatically. |
| Expired but undeleted checkpoint | Generic cleanup-outstanding state; require evidence that expiry was classified on an approved trust basis, plus transaction and fresh absence results where feasible. | Never use the expired checkpoint. If deletion did not commit or absence remains uncertain, retain the outstanding obligation, block access and seek authorized resolution; do not report deletion. |
| Corrupt or legacy record | Generic invalid-record block; preserve sanitized validation category, key/revision availability and transaction outcome. | Do not repair, migrate, infer expiry or automatically discard it. Reviewer determines disposition under a separately approved rule; if key/revision cannot be trusted, define a distinct reviewed deletion procedure before any write. |
| Cleanup transaction failure or conflict | Generic cleanup-failed block; retain sanitized abort/conflict category, expected/current revision result when safely obtainable, and commit/absence evidence. | A new guarded scan may be attempted only as a new request. All waiting operations remain failed; no partial success or deletion claim. Unresolved failure remains an outstanding-retention case. |
| Possible external operation requiring reconciliation | Generic reconciliation-required block; obtain authorized evidence of the actual provider-side outcome through a separately approved inspection path before deciding any new action. | No retry or replay of the uncertain operation. Reconcile and abandon or close the old checkpoint under approved rules; any new workflow requires fresh per-target approval and current account, origin and environment checks. |

**Continuation boundary:** With this browser-only seven-field design, a reviewer cannot establish trusted elapsed time across an unobserved restart from the checkpoint and local clock alone. The recommended rule therefore never resumes an uncertain old checkpoint. A future proposal allowing continuation would require separately approved, independently sufficient time evidence, state/revision and target validation, reconciliation of possible external effects, and a new security decision. A newly approved workflow starts from a new checkpoint; it is not an old-action replay.

If no authorized operator is available, keep the record blocked and locally retained, show a generic pending-review state, and use the owner-approved escalation route when one exists. Until the owner defines that route, record **OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED**; do not invent a contact, deadline or notification channel. A reviewer must account for the physical record until a guarded deletion commits and feasible absence verification finishes. Suspected exposure follows the separate owner-led incident process; deletion must not erase needed incident evidence without authorization. IndexedDB absence does not prove forensic erasure.

## Proposed amendment to Retention Option B — NOT APPROVED

The recommended hold exceeds the current closed-browser exception after an execution with untrusted time or failed cleanup. The owner would need an additional amendment. Proposed wording for separate review:

> When the extension cannot establish expiry on an approved time basis, or a guarded cleanup transaction fails or conflicts, it must block checkpoint use and record an outstanding physical-retention obligation through the approved sanitized process. It must not delete solely on uncertain expiry or treat a failed attempt as deletion. An authorized reviewer may resolve the obligation only under the separately approved manual-disposition procedure, including explicit destructive approval where expiry cannot be established. Any successful deletion requires guarded target/revision checks, transaction completion and feasible absence verification. The checkpoint remains unusable while unresolved. This exception does not establish elapsed time or permit automatic replay.

The owner must separately settle any permitted duration, escalation, authority and incident-evidence treatment before adopting this wording. This draft does not modify the [approved amendment](PHASE_04_SEC01_RETENTION_AMENDMENT_B.md).
