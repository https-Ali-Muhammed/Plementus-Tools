## Conflict and proposed operating rules

Seven-day logical expiry remains `createdAt + 604800000` milliseconds without renewal. Retention Option B requires an attempt to delete expired records at the next permitted extension execution, before checkpoint use. The [clock decision draft](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLOCK_TRUST_DECISION_DRAFT.md), [cleanup design](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_CLOCK_DESIGN.md) and [isolated browser evidence](PHASE_04_SEC01_CLEANUP_GATE_BROWSER.md) do not establish trusted elapsed time across a worker or browser restart. An uncertain clock cannot prove expiry; a forward jump can delete early. A failed or blocked cleanup can leave physical bytes after the first execution. Denial of use, an attempted cleanup and a queued delete are not completed deletion. The current closed-browser exception does not clearly permit a continued hold after execution with unresolved time or transaction failure. **The combined direction therefore requires a separately approved retention amendment before it can operate as policy.**

All rules in this section are **PROPOSED / OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED**:

1. **Block:** On detectable rollback, unexplained forward jump, unresolved restart trust, missing time, invalid/legacy record, cleanup conflict or failure, or possible external write, deny checkpoint read for recovery, transition, external action and expiry-based deletion when expiry is unproved. Show only a generic blocked category and outstanding-cleanup state. A clock reset, plausible local timestamp, operator checkbox or manual approval cannot establish elapsed time. No blocked request resumes automatically.
2. **Temporary retention:** Preserve the blocked local record solely for authorized review, treat it as an outstanding physical-retention obligation, and keep it unavailable. Do not call the hold a completed cleanup. The owner must choose a permitted duration, review/escalation process and disposition when review cannot finish; none is approved here. Until an amendment resolves this conflict, keep production activation NO_GO.
3. **Manual deletion without proven expiry:** Permit only after the owner approves a destructive-disposition rule and a designated decision-maker approves the specific record, reason and expected revision. The owner must decide whether and how to delegate that authority; a general checkbox or blanket approval is insufficient. Approval authorizes disposal, **not** a finding that seven days elapsed. Recheck identity, authority, target and revision inside the guarded transaction; report deletion only after completion and a feasible fresh absence check. A failed absence check remains unresolved. IndexedDB deletion is not forensic erasure.
4. **Evidence before deletion:** The authorized reviewer needs a sanitized case identifier, time-trust or validation failure category, actual service-execution context, record key and expected revision if trustworthy, prior transaction outcome, possible external-write reconciliation result, incident-evidence hold status, reason for disposal and case-specific authorization. Keep this record through an owner-approved process outside the seven-field checkpoint. Do not persist customer data, credentials, URLs, raw errors or screenshots, or expose them in UI/logs.
5. **Conflict, failed transaction, corrupt or legacy row:** An abort or stale revision preserves the row, blocks dependent work and leaves retention outstanding; a later guarded attempt is a new request, never replay of the failed request. Do not infer expiry, repair, migrate or automatically discard malformed/legacy data. If no reliable key/revision exists, do not use the ordinary delete path; the owner must approve a distinct transaction-safe identity/compare procedure before any destructive write. Preserve incident evidence when exposure is suspected.
6. **External effect and new work:** Inspect the actual external outcome through an authorized reconciliation path before deciding any new action. Never retry or replay an uncertain write. The old uncertain checkpoint stays unusable; a new workflow requires fresh per-target approval and current account, origin, environment and permission checks. No continuation of the old checkpoint is proposed without separately approved independent time and state evidence.
7. **Owner unavailable or exposure suspected:** Keep the record blocked and account for its outstanding retention. Use only an owner-approved delegation and escalation route; until defined, no one may assume authority or delete on an untrusted expiry. If exposure is suspected, stop affected actions and preserve sanitized evidence under the existing owner-led incident requirements; an incident hold prevents deletion until the authorized incident process permits it. No contact, channel or deadline is selected here.

The operator would see **checkpoint blocked; authorized review required**, plus generic cleanup **not attempted / unresolved / committed** status. The reviewer would see only the sanitized evidence needed for the case. The extension must not expose record contents, credentials, customer details, raw errors or telemetry. The seven persisted checkpoint fields stay exactly `schemaVersion`, `runId`, `state`, `step`, `evidenceTier`, `revision`, `createdAt`; browser-profile-local storage, the single guarded service, transactional checks and no-replay rules remain unchanged. No backend, remote time service, permission or additional checkpoint field is proposed.

## Maximum-hold feasibility

**No enforceable elapsed-time maximum exists in this browser-only design when time is untrusted.** The seven fields contain no trusted last observation, and a worker restart loses process-local clock comparisons. A changed local clock can make a deadline appear early or late. Chrome may remain closed, the worker may receive no event, and an authorized reviewer may be unavailable. A local alarm, local timestamp or periodic scan cannot prove how long the blocked record has existed or guarantee deletion by a real-time deadline. A maximum can be an owner-set **administrative target** with manual inventory, review and escalation, but it is not a technical guarantee. The owner must either accept that limitation and specify the permitted hold/disposition policy, or keep durable checkpoint activation blocked. This package chooses no numeric limit, monitoring channel or external time source.

## Proposed amendment to Retention Option B — NOT APPROVED

The following text would amend only the treatment of uncertain expiry and unsuccessful cleanup. It does not change seven-day logical expiry, next-execution cleanup when expiry can be established, or the other SEC-01 controls:

> At the first permitted extension execution, the guarded service must attempt to classify and clean expired checkpoints before checkpoint-dependent work. If approved time evidence cannot establish expiry, or cleanup fails or conflicts, it must block checkpoint use and record an outstanding physical-retention obligation through the approved sanitized process. It must not delete on uncertain expiry, treat a failed attempt as completed deletion, or automatically replay work. A blocked record may remain temporarily pending authorized review under the separately approved hold, escalation and disposition rules. If expiry remains unprovable, a designated decision-maker may approve deletion of a specific record as a destructive disposition, without claiming that expiry occurred. Deletion requires approved evidence, guarded identity and revision checks, transaction completion and feasible absence verification; failed or uncertain deletion leaves the obligation open. Suspected-exposure evidence holds take precedence under the approved incident procedure.

**NOT APPROVED:** The owner still must choose the permitted hold period or explicit absence of an enforceable maximum, authority/delegation, evidence and escalation rules. The [approved amendment](PHASE_04_SEC01_RETENTION_AMENDMENT_B.md) is unchanged.
